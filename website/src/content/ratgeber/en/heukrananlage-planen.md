A hay crane system works for twenty, thirty years and longer. That is why it pays to clarify thoroughly before building how it fits the building and the work on the farm. This article summarises which designs there are, how much space the system needs and how the planning works.

## Which design fits?

There is no best design, only one that fits the building. The four common ones:

| Design | How it works | Fits if … |
|---|---|---|
| Monorail crane | The crane travels on a rail under the roof. | the barn is long and narrow and the hay stack lies along the runway. |
| Overhead travelling crane | A crane bridge travels across the full width, the trolley at right angles to it. | the barn is wide and several stacks or unloading points have to be reached. |
| Slewing crane, hydraulic | A slewing tower with a boom travels lengthwise and crosswise and also rotates about its own axis. | the grab should also reach corners, recesses and unloading points beside the runway. |
| Adapted to the building | Runway, supports and span follow the existing building. | the floor plan is unusual or an existing system is being extended. |

Every system includes a grab, a boom, travel units and – for the slewing crane – the slewing tower. How large the grab should be depends on what it picks up and how fast the stack is to grow.

## How much space the system needs

There are no fixed dimensions – every barn is different. These points decide whether and how a system fits:

- **Lifting height:** between the full stack and the roof, there must be room for the grab with its load. The decisive point is the highest point the stack is to reach.
- **Span and travel path:** how wide and how long is the area the crane has to reach – from the unloading point to the furthest corner?
- **Load-bearing capacity:** the crane runway hangs from the roof truss or stands on its own supports. Whether the roof truss can carry the loads must be checked.
- **Unloading point:** where does the fodder arrive, and how does the grab get there without obstacles in the way?
- **Power and controls:** which connection is available, and from where is the crane to be operated – by cable or by radio remote control?

If you have plans or photos of the barn, have them ready for the visit. A measurement of the ridge height, width and length already helps with the first assessment.

## How the planning works

1. **Visit:** we look at the barn, the stack and the unloading point and have you show us how the work is done.
2. **Concept and quote:** you receive a proposal for the design, span and lifting height – with a quote.
3. **Manufacturing:** the system is built at our premises in Tuttwil.
4. **Installation and commissioning:** installation on site, commissioning and instruction in operation.
5. **Service:** season check and maintenance for as long as the system runs.

## When is the right time?

In winter. Then there is enough time for the visit, the quote and manufacturing, and the installation fits into the quiet period before the first cut. If you only start in spring, you often see the season through with the old system.

## And after commissioning?

A new system also needs a crane logbook and regular inspection according to the manufacturer's instructions – what applies is explained in the article [Crane inspection in Switzerland](/ratgeber/kranpruefung-schweiz). A season check before the first cut finds wear before it turns into a breakdown in the middle of the harvest.

Non-binding guidance. The conditions on site and the applicable regulations are decisive.
