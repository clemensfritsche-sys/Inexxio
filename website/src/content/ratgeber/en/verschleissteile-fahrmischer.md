A truck mixer wears where concrete rubs: at the charging hopper when filling, on the mixing spirals inside the drum, on the discharge hopper and chute when emptying. That is not a defect but normal operation. What matters is replacing the parts before they hinder the work or damage adjacent parts.

## How to recognise wear

### Chutes

- The running surface gets thinner; where the concrete always flows the same way, hollows and grooves form.
- Spots worn or rusted through, cracks in welds.
- Worn joints and locks: the chute can no longer be swivelled or secured cleanly.
- Concrete sticks in the hollows and is harder to rinse off.

### Discharge hoppers and charging hoppers

- Washed-out impact surfaces and holes.
- Cracks on fixings and brackets.
- The concrete builds up or no longer flows where it should.

### Mixing spirals and spiral protection

- The spirals in the drum lose height at the edge. With lower spirals the drum mixes less well, empties more slowly and more residual concrete is left behind.
- Spiral protection on the edge absorbs the wear and can be replaced before the spiral itself is affected.
- Whether a spiral is still adequate, we measure against the specifications of the drum manufacturer. The limits differ from drum to drum – which is why we deliberately do not give a figure here.

## When the right time is

- **Chute and discharge hopper:** as soon as the running surface is worn through or a part can no longer be locked safely – not only once the concrete runs over the side.
- **Spirals:** during a drum overhaul. Ideally in winter, when the truck mixer is on the road less anyway and a few days of downtime do not matter. More on this under [Drum replacement in winter](/fahrzeugtechnik/trommeltausch#winter).

## What extends the service life

- Rinse thoroughly after every load, before the concrete sets. Hardened concrete in the drum is extra weight and keeps rubbing.
- Clean the drum from the outside if possible, for example with a high-pressure cleaner. If someone does have to go inside: secure the drive against being switched on again, secure the drum mechanically against turning, ensure safe access. Suva describes this in its guidance on cleaning concrete truck mixers (see sources).
- Check wear parts regularly, not only at the next fault.
