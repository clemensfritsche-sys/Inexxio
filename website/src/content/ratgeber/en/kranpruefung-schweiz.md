A crane is work equipment, and in Switzerland the rule for work equipment is: whoever uses it makes sure it is safe. For cranes, this is set out in detail in the **Crane Ordinance** (SR 832.312.15). For mobile and tower cranes, **FCOS Guideline 6511** also applies (FCOS: Federal Coordination Commission for Occupational Safety). This article summarises what that means in everyday work.

## Who is responsible?

The business that uses the crane is responsible – even if the crane is rented or borrowed. It must ensure that the crane is inspected regularly, that defects are remedied and that everything is recorded in the crane logbook. It may hand the inspection itself over to specialists, but not the responsibility.

## What applies to all cranes?

Whether an overhead travelling crane in a production hall, an underhung crane in an assembly hall or a hay crane in a barn – three duties always apply:

1. **Inspect regularly** – as often and in the way the manufacturer specifies in the operating instructions. The inspection is carried out by trained crane specialists.
2. **Remedy defects** before the crane works again.
3. **Record everything in the crane logbook.** Every crane has a crane logbook. Its form is up to you: a folder in the office or a digital solution – as long as it is complete and available to crane specialists and inspectors.

If the operating instructions for an older crane are missing, it is worth defining the inspection points and intervals once and recording them in writing. Without a specification, an annual inspection is the usual rhythm.

## What also applies to mobile and tower cranes?

For mobile cranes (for example mobile cranes on their own chassis and large lorry loader cranes) and tower cranes, FCOS Guideline 6511 sets out two levels:

- **Annually:** inspection of the safe operating condition by crane specialists.
- **Periodically:** examination by a crane expert recognised by Suva. How often depends on the age of the crane:

| Age of the crane | Examination by a crane expert |
|---|---|
| up to 20 years | every 4 years |
| 21 to 30 years | every 2 years |
| over 30 years | annually |

## Crane specialist or crane expert?

The two terms sound almost the same in German and are often confused. In the crane logbook, however, they stand for two different inspections, with different intervals and different requirements for the person who inspects.

- **Crane specialists** are trained professionals with experience in building, maintaining or repairing cranes. They inspect the safe operating condition – on **all** cranes, regularly and according to the manufacturer's instructions: structure and crane runway, rope or chain and hook, brakes, limit switches and emergency stop, controls and overload protection, travel units.
- **Crane experts** examine **mobile and tower cranes** at the intervals in the table above. They must be recognised by Suva; the requirements are set out in FCOS Guideline 6511.

| Comparison | Crane specialists | Crane experts |
|---|---|---|
| For which cranes | all cranes | mobile and tower cranes |
| How often | according to the manufacturer; for mobile and tower cranes annually | depending on age every 4 years, every 2 years or annually |
| Requirement | training and experience as a crane specialist | recognition by Suva |
| Record | entry in the crane logbook | entry in the crane logbook |

For a **hall crane** (overhead travelling, underhung, jib or slewing crane) and a **hay crane**, an inspection by crane specialists according to the manufacturer's instructions is therefore sufficient; a periodic examination by a crane expert is not required for these cranes.

## What goes into the crane logbook?

The crane logbook contains everything that documents the condition of the crane: the declaration of conformity, every inspection by crane specialists, every examination by a crane expert, plus conversions, major repairs and special events – such as a collision or an overload. If you keep it complete, you can show during an inspection by Suva or in the event of damage that the crane has been looked after.

## What does this mean for a hall crane in your own business?

1. Find the operating instructions and the crane logbook. If one of them is missing: create it now.
2. Set the inspection interval – according to the manufacturer's instructions.
3. Hand the inspection over to crane specialists and record the result in the crane logbook.
4. Set the date for the next time – ideally right away, before it is forgotten.
